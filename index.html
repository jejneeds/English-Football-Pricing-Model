01 · Club brief

Notts County: a possession side that has had to stop having the ball

Notts came up through the League Two play-offs as one of the division's best ball-playing teams. Twelve games into League One, the shape is the same but the game has changed around it. They have 14 points from 15.6 expected, so results are roughly where the performances deserve.

Shape

3-4-2-1 in 8 of 12 league games, a 5-3-2 for the first two, and a 3-4-3 and 3-4-1-2 since. The same back three has carried the side: Bedeau, Ness and Gibbons.

A typical 3-4-2-1 from this season's starters.

What the numbers say

  • The ball has gone. Possession is down from 55% to 44% and passes from 436 to 356 a game. Only two sides play a smaller share of their passes in the opposition half.
  • They have adapted without it. Most tackles in League One and the second-most interceptions. Expected goals against (1.17) is better than average.
  • Chances are the problem. Only 55% of shots come from inside the box (23rd) and they create 1.00 npxG a game (21st).
  • Nothing from the air. Among the fewest aerials won in both seasons (24th, then 23rd) and the second-lowest set-piece xG in League One. They also cross less than almost anyone.
  • No striker output. Their three main forwards have 3.75 npxG between them in about 1,750 minutes. Oliver Cooper, a number ten, has 2.46 on his own.

Why their games are low-scoring at both ends

  • Poor shots both ways. Their own shots are worth 0.086 xG each (21st). The shots they allow are worth 0.089 each, the third-lowest in the league: opponents get volume but not quality.
  • A hard start. Nine of their twelve games have been against the current top half. Against those sides they average 42% possession and are out-shot 12.9 to 11.6. Against the bottom half they have 51% of the ball and win the shot count.
  • When they control games, they dominate them. Home to Burton (2.08 xG to 0.16) and Sheffield Wednesday (2.45 to 0.25) they held the ball and gave almost nothing away.
  • Draws follow. Five draws in twelve, four of them 1-1. With little created at either end, one goal often decides a game or cancels another out.

What to expect: the fixture list softens, so possession and chance creation should recover. The low-quality shots they allow look structural and should hold, which keeps unders in their games live.

Recruitment priorities

  • 1. A striker who lives in the box and wins headers. It fixes the shot quality, the aerial deficit and the set-piece threat at once.
  • 2. A midfielder who keeps the ball, so the possession identity that won promotion survives the step up.
  • 3. A left wing-back who delivers, to give the 3-4-2-1 width and feed priority one.

Shortlisted from League One and Two players with 1,500+ minutes across the last two seasons and age 28 or under (27 for midfield and wing-back), scored on the measures each role needs, then checked by hand.

Why him. Notts have the lowest aerial win rate in League One, their three main strikers average under 0.20 npxG per 90, and only 55% of their shots come from inside the box (23rd). Pressley wins 7.0 aerials a game at 52% (91st percentile) and gets a high-quality chance per shot (0.18 xG, 89th). He is having his best season: 9 goals from 7.0 npxG in 1,058 minutes. He also came through Brentford B, so he knows a structured, data-led club environment.

Projection. Players moving from League Two to League One keep about 84% of their attacking output (92 players, 2022 to 2026). On this season's rate that puts him near 0.50 npxG per 90 in League One, more than double what Notts' current strikers produce.

Risk. Hot streak. His market will move quickly, and last season he scored 7 from 7.7 npxG, so this year is above his norm. A target man also changes how Notts build: they would need to accept more direct balls than their possession model wants.

Why him. Notts were a top-five possession side in League Two (55%, best pass accuracy in the division). In League One that has dropped to 44% (22nd) and they now lead the league for tackles. Read keeps the ball (40 accurate passes per 90 at 81%) and wins 62% of ground duels (94th percentile), so he can help them hold possession without losing the edge out of it. Last season at Colchester he had 7 goals and 7.0 xA in 3,740 minutes. Like Pressley, he is a Brentford B graduate.

Projection. Passing volume carries across divisions better than attacking output, which is why I prioritised it for this role. Expect his xA to drop by about a sixth on the step up.

Risk. Age 27, and only 509 minutes at York this season, so he needs minutes to be sharp. The low minutes are also the opportunity: an out-of-favour player at a League Two club should be gettable in January.

Why him. Notts play 3-4-2-1 but attempt the second-fewest crosses in League One, and their left wing-back spot has rotated between Lipsiuc, Samuels-Smith and Evans. Hancock already plays left back and left wing-back for Walsall, crosses 5.9 times per 90 (89th percentile) and creates 0.17 xA per 90 (90th). This season he has 3 assists from 2.7 xA in 1,077 minutes.

Projection. Age 23 with room to grow. On the 84% carry-over he would still be among the higher-volume creators at wing-back in League One.

Risk. His cross accuracy is ordinary (20%), so the volume is the selling point. Pairing him with an aerial striker like Pressley is what turns that volume into chances.

Also considered: Lee Bonis (Chesterfield) and Jayden Wareham (Exeter) up front, Ryan Stirk (Chesterfield) in midfield, and Jake Batty (Fleetwood) at left wing-back. Stirk scores higher than Read on passing but plays for the League Two leaders, so he is the harder deal. The shortlist was built only from league data; two of the three picks turning out to be Brentford B graduates was a coincidence, and a reassuring one.

Most attacking players lose about an eighth of their output when they move from League One to the Championship (76 players since 2022). Moylan has lost none of it. At Lincoln he played almost entirely as a central number ten. Cardiff have used him there, up front, wide left and in a midfield two, and his box arrivals have actually gone up, from 6.3 to 7.6 touches per 90.

What he is

A penalty-box midfielder: he arrives late, draws fouls (1.96 per 90, 89th percentile) and gets shots away in the area. He creates as well as finishes, with 0.19 xA per 90.

What to watch

He gives the ball away more than most (67% pass accuracy, 11th percentile) and contributes little in the air or in recoveries. His 10 goals include penalties; on 5.1 non-penalty xG he is finishing above expectation, so some cooling is likely even if the underlying output holds.

04 · Case study

Why Brentford paid for Malick Diouf

Diouf played 2,711 Premier League minutes for a West Ham side that went down, aged 21. FotMob now values him at about €34m. Brentford's left side has been covered by Keane Lewis-Potter, a converted winger, who has 8 of their 10 league starts at left back or left wing-back this season.

What Brentford are buying

  • Delivery: high crossing volume (74th percentile) with above-average accuracy (80th).
  • Duels: strong in the air for a full-back (55% won, 72nd) and rarely beaten one against one.
  • Age and minutes: a 21-year-old with a full Premier League season already behind him, bought out of a relegation.

Why he isn't starting yet

Six appearances, one start (Aston Villa) and 185 minutes, mostly as a late substitute. His passing is the gap: 74% accuracy put him in the bottom 5% of Premier League full-backs, and Brentford ask their full-backs to be part of the build-up. My read is a medium-term signing to take the shirt from Lewis-Potter, with the ball-playing side to be coached in.

Checking my own work

Every weekend I score the model against what happened. On 14 November it predicted 26.2% of starters would have a shot on target (actual 26.0%), 58.8% at least one shot (57.7%) and 15.7% would be booked (14.4%). Fouls ran 10% below expectation, which I have flagged to watch rather than chase after one weekend.

A mistake I corrected. That weekend a weaker signal (how teams play when level) was allowed to overrule the main team rating on two games. Both teams it steered away from won. The rule now is that those splits only break ties.
